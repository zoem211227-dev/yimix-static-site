Buying Massage Chairs from a Factory: A Small Business Export Guide

YIMIX Buyer Resources · 21 November 2026

A massage chair export order connects two buyer decisions: what configuration is approved, and what is actually handed to the carrier. The factory-line photograph, container photograph and short video supplied by YIMIX offer a starting point for a discussion about those steps. They show a production setting and a container, but they do not establish the destination, order quantity, inspection outcome or terms of a particular shipment.

Factory container footage provided by YIMIX.

Before you approve the chair

Ask for a model reference and configuration sheet that identify the mechanism, controls, upholstery and accessories you intend to buy. A chair displayed in a production photograph may not match every buyer’s final configuration. Check the destination’s voltage, frequency, plug and documentation requirements with the supplier and a qualified local adviser. Ask which options apply to the selected model and record unanswered questions before paying for a sample or placing an order.

Create a reference for your order

A useful order record links four items: the quotation revision, approved specifications, sample or reference photographs, and the purchase order. If a control panel or finish changes after sample review, write down the change and ask which revised document will be used for production. Keep both the earlier version and the approved update so your receiving and service teams can identify what was ordered.

Connect chair quantities to cartons

Before dispatch, request the number of complete chairs and the number of cartons for each model. Ask whether any leg unit or accessory ships separately, and match carton labels to the packing list. Request carton dimensions and gross weights for your chosen configuration; use those numbers when discussing freight and warehouse handling. A generic container photograph cannot tell you how many chairs fit in your shipment.

For example, if a project lists five chairs and each uses two cartons, the packing record would show ten cartons only if the supplier confirms that packing arrangement for that model. Verify the actual packing list and note any mixed models or missing accessories.

Agree on the handoff evidence

Discuss in advance which photos, inspection records and shipping documents the supplier can provide for your order. The forwarder can confirm booking and transport details; the buyer and supplier should agree who handles insurance, export and import formalities under their written trade terms. At receiving, count cartons, record visible damage and keep the carton labels and photographs alongside the delivery record.

Plan questions for the factory

Send the proposed destination, model references, intended quantity and branding needs. Ask the factory to confirm sample availability, customization scope, packing details, applicable documentation, lead time and after-sales support in writing. Small initial quantities can be discussed, but acceptance and terms depend on the model and project.

Wholesale massage chair orders: turn a shortlist into a shipment

A distributor choosing wholesale massage chairs needs a clear order record that connects the proposed product mix to the cartons and documents received. The photographs below show chairs on production lines and cartons and trucks at a loading area. They are factory and dispatch images provided by YIMIX; they do not establish the quantity, destination, inspection result or terms of your own order.

1. Match the first order to your selling channel

A massage chair retailer does not have to choose a full range before testing customer interest. Start with the channel and the evidence it needs. The examples below are a buyer planning exercise, not a promise that any particular configuration or order size is available.

  • Showroom: ask for the installed and reclined dimensions, demonstration controls and power configuration; check the route from the delivery door to the display position. Ask whether a demonstration or sample unit can be supplied and on what terms.
  • Online store: request model-specific images, dimensions, controller language, packing information and approved product descriptions. Verify which materials you may use and what delivery and assembly information your customers need.
  • Local distributor: identify who will receive cartons, handle customer questions and arrange any service. Ask what information and spare-parts support can be provided for the proposed model and market.

Choose one or two candidate models from the massage chair catalogue and record the reason for each choice. A lower purchase price alone does not show whether the chair suits the channel, local electrical supply or available service arrangements.

2. Compare a proposed trial order with a repeat order

A small business considering five to ten chairs can ask the factory for two written scenarios: an initial test quantity and a possible later reorder. This comparison helps identify differences in configuration, branding and logistics before the first order. Neither scenario establishes an accepted MOQ, a fixed price or a guaranteed reorder schedule.

3. Reconcile the shipment before dispatch

Request a model-by-model packing list: approved model and revision, chair count, cartons per chair, carton markings, gross weight and packed dimensions. Check whether leg units or accessories are separate. Ask which freight responsibilities and export documents are included in the written quotation, and confirm destination requirements with your forwarder. The packaging and shipping guide provides a detailed freight checklist.

4. Give your receiving and service team a reference

Keep the agreed specifications, sample reference, quotation, packing list and shipment photographs together. At arrival, count cartons, document visible damage and match accessories to each chair. Ask about spare-parts identification and written warranty or support terms before you agree the order.
