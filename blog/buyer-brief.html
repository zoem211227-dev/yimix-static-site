How to Write a Massage Chair Sourcing Brief

Give a supplier enough information to review your proposed order without turning your preferences into assumed specifications or commercial promises.

YIMIX Buyer Resources · Updated 9 November 2026

A useful first brief answers five questions: which chair, how many of each model, where it will be delivered, how it will be used, and what you want the supplier to confirm. You do not need a complete product specification to start. Mark unknown items as questions instead of filling them with guesses.

What to include · Copy-ready example · Review the first reply · Before you send

Include the information that changes the supplier’s answer

1. Your business, market and intended use

Say whether you are an importer, retailer, distributor, online seller or brand. Name the destination country and explain the installation: resale for home use, a showroom, a workplace or a commercial location. A product photo alone does not explain the intended operating conditions. If the use is undecided, start with the home versus commercial buying checklist.

2. A model reference and a short list of priorities

Link the product page or attach a clearly named reference image. Write “reference only” when you have not approved the configuration. Separate must-have requirements from preferences such as colour, upholstery or controller language. Ask the supplier to identify the exact proposed model and any substitution.

For a starting point, compare the listed model specifications or browse the mid-range collection. A request about the ZZG12 model should use that model’s linked specification rather than copying a feature list from another chair. Appearance, a category label or a mechanism name is not a complete specification.

3. The proposed quantity for each model

Write an exact planning quantity. If you hope to start with 5–10 chairs, say that it is a proposed test order and ask whether the selected model is available on that basis. YIMIX’s acceptance, model MOQ and any conditions require written confirmation. Do not describe a target quantity as an agreed minimum.

For mixed models, list the split: for example, “four of reference A and four of reference B, subject to your confirmation.” Ask about each model’s minimum, colour and branding conditions. Keep your expected repeat order separate from the initial request; a possible future order is not a purchase commitment.

4. Destination, electrical requirements and delivery access

Provide the destination city and country, plus a port or postcode if known. Say whether you need a shipment to a port, warehouse or another delivery point. Mention stairs, lifts, narrow doors or a separate leg unit if they could affect delivery or assembly. Ask the supplier to confirm complete packing dimensions and carton count rather than using the chair’s assembled size for logistics.

State the voltage, frequency and plug you require, or ask for confirmation if you do not know. List the documents you need for the selected model and destination; do not assume a general factory certificate establishes every model’s suitability. Use the packing and shipping checklist to identify information still needed.

5. Sample, branding, timing and support questions

Say whether you need a sample review before deciding on a larger order. Ask for sample charges, freight, the proposed configuration and any credit conditions in writing. For branding, distinguish a logo request from a new appearance, material or function requirement. The OEM/ODM scope checklist helps you explain the difference without assuming that customization is included.

Give your desired arrival date as a target and ask the supplier to confirm feasibility, production timing and the approval trigger. Request written warranty coverage, exclusions, spare-parts arrangements and responsibility for parts freight. This brief collects questions; it does not establish YIMIX’s sample, lead-time or after-sales policy.

Give every shortlisted model a job in the trial

Use one line per model in the initial order plan. Include the sales channel, proposed units, the buyer question the trial should answer and the evidence you want before placing a repeat order. For a zero gravity showroom model, the question might be whether it can recline in your demo space. For an online SL track model, it might be whether the listing team can explain the verified configuration and delivery requirements from the sample and its documentation.

If you want to evaluate two models in a five to ten unit trial, show the proposed split and ask the supplier to confirm the accepted configuration, quantity, sample terms and timing in writing. Keep a separate approval line for each model. Use the retail assortment guide if two models appear to serve the same customer need.

Copy-ready first-order brief

Hypothetical planning example, not an accepted order. Replace the brackets and keep anything unconfirmed labelled “please confirm.” Remove requests that do not apply to your project.

Business: [retailer / importer / distributor / online seller / brand]
Market and intended use: [country; home resale / showroom / other]
Model or reference link: [ ]; reference only, not yet approved
Must-have requirements: [ ]; preferences: [ ]
Proposed first order: eight chairs of [model], subject to confirmation
Alternative mixed-model request, only if needed: [model + quantity each]
Destination: [city, country, port or postcode if known]
Delivery point and access: [warehouse / port / other; known restrictions]
Electrical requirements: [voltage / frequency / plug, or please confirm]
Branding: [unbranded / logo / packaging / other request]
Sample: [needed before order decision / please advise]
Desired arrival: [target date, subject to confirmed timing]
Please confirm: model configuration, applicable MOQ, complete packing,
quotation scope, sample terms, timing and written support arrangements.
Open questions: [ ]

If you have not chosen a model, replace the model field with your intended use and two or three priorities. Asking for a recommendation is more useful than naming a configuration you have not checked. Keep an internal copy and date each revision so later replies refer to the same request.

Read a supplier's first reply without treating it as approval

A useful brief gives the supplier a way to answer the questions that affect your buying decision. After the first reply, separate three things: what you requested, what the supplier is proposing and what has actually been confirmed for the named configuration. Keep your original requirements visible. A reply such as “customization available” does not identify the controller language, artwork position or quantity basis you asked about.

Use a short response check rather than resending an expanding feature list. Give each open question an owner on your side and a reference in the supplier's reply. If an essential requirement has not been addressed, ask about that requirement directly. Do not remove it from the brief merely to make the exchange look complete.

Example: eight chairs, two references and one essential requirement

Suppose an independent retailer proposes four chairs from reference A and four from reference B. English-language operation is essential for its staff; a particular carton colour is a preference. The buyer sends both references, the destination and that distinction in the first brief. Neither the quantity nor either requested change has been accepted yet.

If the reply proposes only one reference and mentions branding generally, the buyer has two practical questions: does the proposed configuration address the language requirement, and can the original model split be considered? Ask the supplier to mark an alternative as an alternative, with the differences identified. Do not carry a controller statement from one chair across to the other.

The retailer can then decide whether to review the proposed alternative, revise the optional artwork request or continue looking for a configuration that meets its essential requirement. Save the revised brief with a date and show what changed. This is an illustration of an enquiry decision, not an accepted eight-chair order, a model MOQ or a promise that an English controller can be supplied.

Move to comparison only when the reply is specific enough

Once each proposed model and remaining question is identifiable, use the quotation comparison guide to review the offered scope. Until then, comparing headline figures can hide differences in what is being offered. The brief is your request for review; a quotation, sample approval and purchase order are later decisions with their own confirmed references.

Check the brief before sending

  • Does every reference identify a model or clearly say “reference only”?
  • Do the quantities per model add up to your proposed initial order?
  • Are your destination, electrical questions and delivery point visible?
  • Have you separated required features from optional branding or appearance preferences?
  • Are sample credits, mixed-model acceptance, timing and support written as questions until confirmed?
  • Have you listed the information you still need rather than treating blanks as agreement?

Once replies arrive, use the supplier quote comparison worksheet to resolve differences. Before approving a sample or production reference, use the sample inspection checklist. These are separate decisions: sending a brief is not approving the product or placing an order.
