Massage Chair Product Information Checklist for Retailers

A wholesale catalogue is a starting point, not a finished retail listing. Prepare a model-specific information pack that answers customer questions without adding features or service promises that your supply agreement does not support.

YIMIX Buyer Resources · Published 20 October 2026 · Updated 7 November 2026

In this guide

  2. Describe functions in language a customer can check
  3. Separate product size, access and delivery service
  4. RS1178: turn source facts into a retail draft

  6. Review the pack before and after launch

Build one approved source for each listing

Choose a named model and record the exact configuration you plan to sell. Separate the supplier’s listed information from questions still awaiting an answer. Keep the approval date and the person responsible for your final listing so your website, showroom card and sales team use consistent information.

Do not copy specifications across similar-looking models. A common shell, product category or rail description does not establish identical programmes, heating areas or controls. If a field is missing, ask for clarification instead of filling it from a competing retailer’s page.

Describe functions in language a customer can check

Translate an approved function into a clear description of what the user selects or experiences, without inventing a performance claim. If a mechanism label is ambiguous, request its model-specific meaning. Keep optional configurations distinct from what is included in the advertised unit.

Avoid medical treatment claims and unsupported comparisons. A supplier image or a generic feature name is not evidence that a chair treats a condition or outperforms another product. Keep technical questions connected to the agreed specification and any appropriate supporting documents.

Separate product size, access and delivery service

Label upright dimensions, packaging dimensions and any confirmed reclined footprint separately. A carton measurement is not a doorway guarantee. Identify information still needed for access, handling and installation before offering a delivery service to a customer.

Describe only the service you have arranged: who delivers, where the handoff takes place and who handles setup questions. Factory packing and overseas freight do not automatically include final-mile installation. Refer unresolved service details to the responsible provider before publishing a promise.

A useful information pack shows both what your source says and what is still missing. The current RS1178 product page provides a practical example. The values below are published reference information, not confirmation of a customized order or approval to launch a particular retail listing. Ask the factory to confirm the configuration you will actually sell.

A short product description you can review with the factory

Draft for review: RS1178 Premium Massage Chair. The published reference lists 220 V / 50 Hz electrical requirements, 110 W rated power and a maximum user load of 150 kg. Main-unit package dimensions are 129 × 78 × 113 cm; published net and gross weights are 75 kg and 92 kg. Confirm the supplied configuration and final packing before ordering.

This paragraph deliberately leaves out unconfirmed massage mechanisms, programme counts, installed dimensions and treatment claims. Your customer-facing listing still needs approved photographs of the ordered configuration, operating information and the delivery service you have arranged. Keep it as an internal draft until the missing essentials are resolved; do not turn an unanswered field into a selling point.

Adapt the handoff to your sales channel

For an online store, check that the title, specification table, image gallery and delivery note describe the same configuration. For a showroom, use the same approved source in the display card and staff explanation. If you are discussing a small-batch branded order, record which logo, colour, controller or packaging requests are proposed and which have written confirmation. A proposed change does not automatically change the published specifications.

Send a focused request: model and proposed quantity; destination market; sales channel; fields awaiting confirmation; image or manual references; and the decision needed before your listing goes live. Use the existing Buyer Brief for the project details. If the sample differs from the proposed listing, record that difference using the sample inspection checklist before approving the retail information. Keep quotation scope and sample acceptance in their own records rather than repeating them in the product description.

Worked example: an image shows a different control panel

Suppose the approved specification names one controller but the supplied lifestyle image shows another. Flag the image against the listing record and ask whether it represents the proposed configuration. Do not silently keep it because the chair colour matches.

Request an appropriate image or a clear explanation, and confirm permission for your intended use. Retain any agreed restrictions or required credit. AI illustrations can explain a purchasing topic, but should be labelled and should not replace accurate product photographs in a configuration claim.

Approve visual assets for the channels where you will sell

Matching a photograph to a chair is only the first check. A retailer also needs to know where that photograph or video may be used, what changes are acceptable and which configuration it represents. Keep this information beside the asset reference so a colleague preparing a marketplace listing does not assume that a file received in a quotation can be reused without clarification. This is a practical purchasing record, not legal advice or a statement that YIMIX grants a particular licence.

Ask for a channel-specific asset handoff

Describe your intended use before requesting files: your own online store, a third-party marketplace, a printed showroom card or paid advertising. Ask the supplier to identify the assets available for that use and confirm any restrictions, required credit or limits on modification. Discuss cropping, translated overlays and your proposed branding separately. A supplier's ability to share a photograph does not by itself establish permission for every person, brand mark, soundtrack or location visible in it.

Record a stable file reference, the model and configuration shown, the intended channel, the permitted changes and the written answer. Do not infer exclusivity from a logo change or from being the first person to receive a file. If exclusivity matters to your project, raise it explicitly and obtain a written response rather than presenting it as an included service.

Use a release record instead of an unlabelled image folder

  • Asset identity: file name or reference, view shown and date received.
  • Product match: model, colour, controller and included accessories visible in the asset.
  • Use request: sales channel, territory or language where relevant to your question, and proposed alterations.
  • Confirmation: the reply that answers the use request, any unresolved conditions and the person responsible for release.
  • Published locations: the listing, brochure or display using that asset, so it can be replaced consistently.

Example: one photograph is suitable for the shop but unresolved for an advertisement

Imagine that a retailer has a confirmed product photograph for its own shop page and later wants to place the same image in a paid campaign with a new logo overlay. The earlier answer may not cover that use. Keep the shop asset and campaign request separate, ask about the proposed channel and modification, and leave the campaign asset pending until the answer is clear. This example does not establish an actual restriction or permission on any YIMIX photograph.

If the ordered chair changes colour or controller, repeat the product-match check even when the permission answer has not changed. Conversely, replacing a background does not confirm a new product configuration. Keep asset permission, specification approval and customer instruction review as separate decisions. For operating instructions, use the controller usability review; do not treat a promotional video as an operating manual.

Make the clarification request easy to answer

Send the model, exact file references, proposed channel and specific edit you want to make. Attach a clearly labelled internal mock-up only where appropriate, and ask for an answer to the unresolved use rather than requesting a vague marketing pack. Use the buyer brief for the wider purchasing context. Confirm available material and project terms with the team; do not publish a promise of exclusive images, free custom videos or unrestricted rights without written agreement.

Review the pack before and after launch

Check the title, feature list, images, manual reference, dimension labels and delivery wording against the same configuration. Have someone unfamiliar with the draft read it and list unanswered questions. A useful pack should make those gaps obvious before a customer orders.

When the supply configuration changes, review every place where the old information is used. Record customer questions separately from confirmed product updates. Your listing is not improved by adding popular search phrases if those phrases make the product less accurately described.

Approve each sellable SKU before publishing colour or controller options

A factory model name and a retailer's SKU have different jobs. The model identifies the supplier reference; your SKU should let your sales and fulfilment team identify the exact version being offered. A colour swatch, a different controller photograph and a request for another electrical version should not be combined into selectable options until the relevant combinations have been confirmed.

This matters for an importer preparing its first retail range. A customer may select a colour on the product page while the warehouse follows a separate stock label. If those references do not match, an accurate feature description still cannot tell the team which unit to dispatch. Keep an internal mapping before making a variant available for ordering. This is a suggested retailer workflow, not a statement that YIMIX uses your SKU system.

Example: an image gallery shows more options than the order includes

A small brand receives images showing a dark chair with one control panel and a light chair with another. Its confirmed purchase covers only one configuration. The listing team should use the confirmed version as the sale item and keep the other combination as an internal question. Do not publish the second image as a selectable, available variant merely because the shell looks the same. Ask which finish, controller and instructions apply before approving that additional SKU.

Check the matrix whenever a repeat order changes the finish, controller or electrical reference. Update the images and customer information that actually change; keep unchanged references identifiable. For retailers who also use Google Merchant Center, Google's variant grouping guidance says the product details submitted should match the landing page. That conditional example is not a claim that YIMIX operates a Merchant Center feed or that grouping products guarantees search visibility.

Use the controller review for instructions and the electrical configuration checklist for destination questions. Send the factory your proposed SKU-to-model mapping and only the combinations you want confirmed. Small-batch customization availability, mixed-model acceptance, quantities, charges and production timing still require a written response for the project.
